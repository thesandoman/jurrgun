# BKK Social \- City Values Quiz Question Bank

## Scenario-based civic / city-value questions for BMA mobile web

# Purpose

This quiz is designed to feel like a light Bangkok lifestyle / city-choice quiz rather than an overt political questionnaire. The goal is to understand how residents think about trade-offs in urban life \- safety, freedom, public space, development, spending, inclusion and participation \- while keeping the user experience playful and relevant to BKK Social.

Important guardrail: the wording can be subtle, but the product should still be transparent in its privacy notice that responses may be used to understand civic values and city-policy preferences. The quiz should not secretly infer or label party affiliation, voting history or ideology.

# Target Audience

• Primary audience: Bangkok residents aged 20-35.  
• Open to all adults, with age available for segmentation.  
• Analysis may distinguish people currently living in Bangkok from verified Bangkok-registered residents.  
• Recommended use: optional City Values / City Pulse module inside BKK Social, not a mandatory dating-profile field.

# Recommended Scoring Dimensions

• Community / preservation ↔ Growth / development  
• Freedom / informality ↔ Order / regulation  
• Collective provision ↔ Individual / user-pays  
• Citizen-led ↔ Expert / authority-led  
• Traditional / continuity ↔ Open-to-change / pluralism  
• Car / convenience ↔ People / public-space orientation

Do not show users a simplistic 'left vs right' score. A better output is a friendly city-personality type, e.g. 'The Social Urbanist', 'The Neighborhood Keeper', 'The Practical Builder' or 'The Free-Spirit Explorer'.

# Question Design Principles

• Use real Bangkok situations, not ideological labels.  
• Make both choices sound reasonable.  
• Prefer trade-offs over agree/disagree statements.  
• Use forced-choice, ranking, sliders and small budget-allocation tasks.  
• Avoid questions that directly ask party support, vote choice or candidate preference.  
• Mix categories so users do not see the underlying scoring dimension.  
• Use 12-20 questions per quiz session; rotate from a larger question bank.

# Question Bank

## A. Everyday Bangkok Choices

1\. You have a free Sunday. Which Bangkok sounds better?

• A. Streets closed for walking, markets and activities  
• B. Roads kept fully open so getting around is easier

2\. A new café street becomes very popular, but local rent starts rising. What would concern you more?

• A. The original community disappearing  
• B. The area losing momentum if development is restricted

3\. A road near your home can fit only one improvement. Pick one.

• A. Wider sidewalk \+ trees  
• B. More parking / traffic capacity

4\. Which makes a neighborhood feel more alive?

• A. Lots of small shops, vendors and different things happening  
• B. Clean, organized spaces with clear rules

5\. Would you rather live somewhere that is...

• A. A little messy but full of character  
• B. Very organized but more predictable

6\. A park has become very crowded in the evening. What should happen first?

• A. Add more activities and facilities  
• B. Introduce more rules to control use

7\. What makes a city feel successful to you?

• A. People actually enjoying everyday life there  
• B. Big projects and strong economic activity

• A. A slightly cheaper commute every day  
• B. One really good new public attraction in your district

10\. A public service costs more than expected. What sounds fairer?

• A. Everyone contributes so the price stays low  
• B. People who use it more should pay more

11\. If the city could subsidize only one thing, what feels most useful?

• A. Getting around  
• B. Housing / living costs  
• C. Community activities

12\. Which feels like better use of public money?

• A. Improvements almost everyone notices a little

• A. The people already living and working there

15\. What bothers you more?

• A. A neighborhood changing too quickly

• A. Small public space \+ community uses

17\. When an area becomes popular, who should benefit first?

• A. People already there

## D. Freedom, Rules and Safety

• A. A place with too few rules  
• B. A place with too many rules

• A. More people around  
• B. More security / police around

22\. Two groups want to use the same public square: one for a quiet community activity and one for a loud public event. What should matter more?

• A. Freedom to use public space

## E. Diversity and Social Attitudes

• A. More interesting  
• B. More difficult to live in

24\. At a city event, would you rather be grouped with...

• A. People quite similar to you  
• B. People you normally would never meet

25\. What makes Bangkok feel more 'Bangkok'?

• A. Keeping its traditional character

26\. Which city sounds more attractive?

• A. Everyone can live quite differently without bothering each other  
• B. People share stronger common norms about how to behave

27\. If a public campaign represented many different types of families and relationships, your reaction would be...

• Scale: Very uncomfortable → Completely normal

28\. Which is more important for a city?

• A. Making everybody feel included

## F. Who Should Make Decisions?

29\. Your district has THB 50 million to improve something. Which process sounds better?

• A. Residents vote on several options  
• B. Experts study the area and choose

30\. If a project is unpopular at first but experts strongly believe it will work long-term, should Bangkok...

• A. Try it anyway  
• B. Wait until more residents support it

31\. Who usually understands a neighborhood best?

• A. People who live there  
• B. Professionals who study the whole city

32\. Bangkok has 50 districts. Should they feel...

• A. More consistent, with similar standards everywhere

• A. Majority decides

## G. Environment without 'Environmental Politics' Language

• Scale: Definitely no → Definitely yes

35\. If a busy street became pedestrian-only every weekend, your first reaction would be...

• A. Finally  
• B. Sounds fun, but traffic will be terrible

• A. 30 additional parking spaces

• Scale: 1-5

38\. A street floods frequently. Which solution feels better?

• A. Major engineering infrastructure

# Better Response Formats than Agree / Disagree

• Forced choice: 'Which would you choose if Bangkok could only have ONE?'  
• Priority trade-off: 'Which bothers you MORE?'  
• Budget game: give users 10 coins to divide among transport, public space, cost of living, safety, jobs/business and culture.  
• Ranking: drag 5 city priorities from most to least important.  
• Scenario slider: choose where you sit between two reasonable options.

# Suggested Result Experience

Do not output '68% liberal / 32% conservative'. A better user-facing result is a friendly city archetype based on multiple dimensions.

• The Social Urbanist \- lively, walkable, inclusive, public-space focused.  
• The Neighborhood Keeper \- community continuity, local identity, cautious development.  
• The Practical Builder \- infrastructure, delivery, efficiency, visible results.  
• The Free-Spirit Explorer \- openness, informality, nightlife, experimentation.  
• The Local Voice \- participation, district autonomy, community decision-making.  
• The Orderly Optimist \- safety, predictability, standards and structured improvement.

## Example Result Copy

Your Bangkok Type: The Social Urbanist

You like a Bangkok that is lively, walkable and open to different people. You do not mind change, but you want development to improve everyday life \- not just the skyline.

# Recommended Quiz Structure

• 12-20 questions per session.  
• Rotate from a larger bank so repeat users do not always see the same questions.  
• Mix categories randomly.  
• Keep average completion time under 4 minutes.  
• Use one or two playful questions for every three policy trade-off questions.  
• Save raw answers separately from any derived score.  
• Do not use the score to match users romantically.  
• If used for research segmentation, apply minimum group-size thresholds before displaying results in the BMA dashboard.

# Internal Analytics Note

Each answer can map to one or more hidden analytical dimensions, but the scoring model should be documented internally and validated before it is used for policy conclusions. The product should describe the quiz as a city-values / civic-preference exercise rather than as a diagnostic of a person's political identity.

# BKK Type System & Compatibility Logic (MBTI-style)

## Concept

Turn the City Values score into a memorable 4-letter BKK Type, similar in spirit to MBTI, but based on city values rather than personality. The letters should be a fun shorthand; the user-facing output should lead with a friendly archetype name.

## The 4 Dimensions

1\. N / G — Neighborhood vs Growth  
N \= Neighborhood-first: community, local identity, preservation, everyday quality of life.  
G \= Growth-first: development, investment, change, ambitious city projects.

2\. F / O — Freedom vs Order  
F \= Free-flow: spontaneity, nightlife, flexible rules, informal city life.  
O \= Organized: safety, predictability, standards, structure.

3\. V / E — Voice vs Expert  
V \= Voice-led: residents should have more say, local participation, bottom-up decisions.  
E \= Expert-led: professionals and planners should make difficult long-term decisions.

4\. P / C — People-space vs Convenience  
P \= People-first city: walking, parks, public space, transit and street life.  
C \= Convenience-first city: speed, parking, efficient movement and practical access.

## Compatibility Labels

Natural Match  
Similar fundamental city values and an easy sense of "we see Bangkok in a similar way."

Complementary Match  
Different approaches, but the differences can balance one another and create useful chemistry.

Interesting Match  
Meaningfully different viewpoints that may create a good conversation rather than an automatic incompatibility.

Important product rule: do not frame any pair as "bad", "wrong" or "incompatible". Difference should be presented as curiosity and conversation potential. The quiz should not be used to exclude people from events or silently rank romantic suitability.

## Recommended Compatibility Logic

A simple implementation should not rely only on exact type pairs. Each pair can receive a hidden conversation-compatibility score using:

• 40% Shared city values — enough similarity for common ground.  
• 30% Complementarity — one meaningful difference can make the interaction more interesting.  
• 20% Social / activity compatibility — shared interests such as food, running, art, cafés, volunteering or nightlife.  
• 10% Conversation novelty — enough difference to create something to talk about.

A useful rule of thumb is that matching on 3 of the 4 City Values dimensions and differing on 1 can often be stronger than matching on all 4\.

## How to Use This in BKK Social

• Show the archetype after the City Values quiz.  
• Let users optionally display the type on their BKK Social profile.  
• Use it to generate conversation prompts and event icebreakers.  
• Allow users to opt in to seeing "Natural", "Complementary" and "Interesting" connections.  
• Do not use City Values as a hard romantic-match filter.  
• Do not hide or suppress people because their city values differ.  
• Keep the underlying political/civic scoring private unless the user chooses to see more detail.  
• If the system recommends a person based partly on City Values, explain that the recommendation is about conversation fit / shared city outlook, not political compatibility.
